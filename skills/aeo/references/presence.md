# Presence, consensus and accuracy

Being cited and being recommended are different outcomes. A citation means a page was useful to quote. A recommendation means the engine put the brand on the buyer's shortlist, and that rests mostly on what the rest of the web says about it.

## The ladder

| Rung | Meaning | What earns it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieved | The engine read the page | Crawl access, indexing, parseable HTML |
| Cited | The page is listed as a source | A passage that answers the question, with evidence |
| Mentioned | The brand is named in the answer | The engine knows the brand as an entity |
| Recommended | The brand is on the shortlist | Agreement across reviews, forums, press, analysts and video |

Report each rung separately, and note the framing of each mention: recommended, neutral, hedged, or recommended against. A rising citation count with a flat recommendation rate means the web does not yet back the site's claims.

Test before funding another self-ranked "best X" guide: if the engine ignored the brand's own domain, would the rest of the web still put it on the shortlist? In one 2026 study of 100 B2B "best software" queries, 69% of the AI Overview citations that self-promotional listicles earned came in answers that recommended competitors instead. A fairer guide names the category leader first, says what it does well, then shows where this product goes further.

## Entity signals

- One canonical name, description and category, used the same way on the About page, `Organization` schema, and every profile.
- `Organization` JSON-LD on the home page with `name`, `url`, `logo`, `description` and `sameAs` links to the official profiles (LinkedIn, GitHub, Crunchbase, Wikipedia or Wikidata where they exist). The **seo** skill owns the markup itself.
- Third-party profiles (G2, Capterra, app stores, Crunchbase, LinkedIn, partner directories, Google Business Profile) filled in completely with the same segment and positioning. An afternoon of edits, and engines read them.

## Earned presence

Recommendations follow consensus, so work where the engines look for the category. Classify the sources engines cite for the tracked prompts first (own site, competitor sites, editorial, institutional, review platforms, forums and social), split by engine, then spend effort where the citations are.

- **Review platforms**: real customer reviews, asked for at the right moment.
- **Editorial and analysts**: press, industry roundups, analyst reports. The pages cited for the prompts are the outreach list.
- **Communities**: Reddit, Hacker News, niche forums, Discord. Take part honestly under a real name, only where engines cite them. Citation mixes shift overnight, so never rely on one surface.
- **Video and podcasts**: engines read the transcript, title, description and chapters, not the video.
- **Wikipedia**: only when the brand is notable by Wikipedia's rules, and never edited by the brand itself.

Positioning moves slowly. A brand known for one segment does not get known for another because its pages change. Before a repositioning, rebrand or merger, record baseline prompts for both segments, update the off-site profiles first, and keep pages that still win the old prompts until tracking shows the new association.

## When an engine gets a fact wrong

1. Capture the answer: engine, date, locale, exact prompt, and the sources it cited.
2. Log each claim that could change a decision: price, availability, compatibility, feature, limit, comparison. Mark it accurate, incomplete, ambiguous, stale, false, opinion stated as fact, or unverifiable, and rate the severity by harm and by how often it appears.
3. Trace the claim to the page it came from.
4. Fix owned sources first: state the correct fact plainly, with a date, on the page that should own it, and redirect or update stale pages, feeds and structured data.
5. Fix stale profiles, then send third parties the correct fact with a source. Ask only for factual corrections, never for fair criticism to be removed.
6. Retest the same prompts after 4 to 8 weeks. Models re-retrieve and retrain on their own schedule, so never promise an answer will change.

## Out of bounds

Fake reviews, invented awards, brand-run "independent" review sites, astroturfed forum posts, paid mentions presented as editorial, and editing Wikipedia about the brand. They break platform rules and the FTC's 2024 rule on reviews and testimonials, and engines are learning to discount them. Google says artificial mentions do not help.
